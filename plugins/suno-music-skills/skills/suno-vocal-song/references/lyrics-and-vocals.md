# Lyrics and Vocals

Use for writing or materially revising lyrics. Write in the requested language and native script; genre does not override language. A translation is a separate review artifact.

Build a perspective, concrete imagery, an emotional movement, and singable phrases. Repetition can anchor a hook; the user may prefer through-composed forms, spoken delivery, rap, a duet, or no chorus. Match structure to the request rather than imposing an eight-section form.

Section cues such as `[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, and `[Bridge]` can clarify the plan. Sparse delivery cues may distinguish a whispered verse from a belted chorus, but neither brackets nor punctuation guarantee delivery. Do not rely on `~word~`, trailing hyphens, all caps, or inline tags as exact musical controls.

Use rhythm and natural phrasing to judge line length. The old 6–12 English syllables / 8–15 Korean syllables / 10–18 Japanese mora ranges were untested estimates, not language rules. Do not pad or cut lines solely to match them. There is no established universal character boundary at which Suno truncates a song.

Describe vocal identity, range, texture, delivery, and language only as needed. Preserve requested mixed voices rather than forcing every song into a male/female binary selector. Where a selector is available, align it with the description; an account Voice or source recording can also influence the result.

Keep the copyable Lyrics block free of translations and global mixing notes. User-provided lyrics can be revised; do not reconstruct a named song's copyrighted lyrics from memory. To lengthen an existing good recording, consider an Extend instruction before padding verses.
