# Instrumentation and Arrangement

Use for composing or materially changing an instrumental. Identify the motif or evolving texture, supporting harmony, bass, and rhythmic role where applicable. Two to four instruments can start a simple request; a solo piano or full orchestra is equally valid when intended.

Technique can be more informative than an instrument name: fingerpicking, muted strings, volume swells, tremolo, or long sustained notes. Include a technique wherever it makes an audible difference, including a supporting part.

Save the section plan independently of Lyrics. Four to six sections is one useful song shape, not a minimum or maximum. A short logo may need only one gesture; an ambient piece may evolve continuously. Bar counts and timestamps are targets, not scheduling commands.

For study or background music, consider restrained dynamics and gradual transitions. Do not assume every focus listener wants no drums or the same BPM. A dramatic peak is appropriate when requested.

A fade-out can suit a standalone ending but does not create a seamless loop. For a loop request, ask for compatible entry/exit texture and pulse, then require listening or waveform editing to verify the join. `[loop-friendly]` alone is experimental.

Use Instrumental ON for no voices. If the UI accepts section cues in Lyrics, that can be tried; otherwise put the arrangement in Styles/Prompt and keep Lyrics empty. For requested humming or wordless choir use OFF and describe the intended texture, without promising that Suno cannot invent words.

Use exclusions to remove genuinely unwanted elements. Do not exclude requested choir, and do not automatically fill the space left by removed drums or instruments. Judge the actual generation before diagnosing contamination.
